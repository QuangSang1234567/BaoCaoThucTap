## Reflections and future direction

### Strengths developed during learning

I followed a systematic learning path, from IAM and VPC networking to servers, storage, databases, authentication, monitoring and deployment.

I learned to bring together the roles of services rather than simply memorize their names; for example, relating IAM roles to application permissions and Cognito Access Tokens to API protection.

I related AWS knowledge to relevant CloudDocs topics such as user authentication, API access and document storage.

I completed a weekly worklog documenting objectives, activities and learning outcomes, which helped me recognize gaps in my knowledge.

### Areas for improvement

AWS service practice: I need to configure small labs myself to better understand IAM policies, VPCs, Security Groups, EC2, S3 and database connections.

Security and authentication: I need practice checking Access Token attributes and how APIs validate signatures, issuers, expiration and client/audience in the appropriate Cognito context.

Operations and deployment: I need practice reading logs, setting alarms, troubleshooting and trying deployments with Lambda, Systems Manager, CloudFormation, Docker or CI/CD.

Problem-solving approach: I should record symptoms, hypotheses, investigation steps and results, changing one factor at a time to identify causes clearly.

Evidence and technical communication: I should save configurations, diagrams, screenshots and executed commands; reports should clearly state objectives, results and unfinished work.

### Direction after the internship

After the internship, I want to continue developing in Backend and Cloud. My immediate goal is to practice deploying a small CloudDocs workflow: users sign in through Cognito, the API validates Access Tokens, and the application controls access and stores files in S3. I will also add integration tests, log monitoring and cost tracking to gain a fuller understanding of operations.
