Personal reflections after 12 internship weeks

Student: Hán Quang Sang    Class: K68CNTTA

After 12 weeks in the First Cloud AI Journey program, I see my learning as a systematic introduction to AWS and how cloud services support an application. My worklog progressed from accounts, costs and IAM to VPC networking, EC2, S3, RDS, Cognito, monitoring, automation and deployment. I related some topics to CloudDocs while recognizing that I need more practice to turn foundational knowledge into practical work skills.

## Assessment by aspect

### 1. Internship environment

The 12-week path helped me move from basic concepts to topics connected within a system. Studying by topic and recording outcomes in my worklog helped me track my progress. Since much of the work involved independent reading and revision, I also had to manage my time proactively to keep up.

### 2. Mentoring and support

Guidance materials and community discussions gave me starting points when topics were unclear. During my learning, I found that questions accompanied by configurations, error messages and steps already attempted helped others understand the problem faster. This is a skill I want to keep developing through project work.

### 3. Relevance to Information Technology

The program topics connect with computer networking, databases and web programming that I studied at university. Relating them to CloudDocs helped me better understand IAM for authorization, Cognito for user authentication, Access Tokens for API calls, and S3 and RDS for file and data storage.

### 4. Skills where I noticed the most improvement

I can distinguish the purposes of services such as IAM, VPC, EC2, S3, RDS, Cognito, CloudWatch and Lambda.

I better understand the connections between identities, access permissions, networking, data and APIs when considering an application.

I have begun separating problems into layers such as permissions, network connectivity, application configuration and data; however, I need more labs to develop root-cause investigation skills.

I have improved at keeping a worklog, consolidating information and preparing supporting evidence.

### 5. Community culture

Attending the Mini Meetup – First Cloud AI Journey and following shared content introduced me to more perspectives on cloud, AI and career development. I found community discussions useful for beginners, especially for understanding how AWS knowledge is used at work.

### 6. Learning conditions and organization

Online materials and the AWS Console allowed me to study at suitable times. Through AWS Budgets and resource management topics, I also understood the need to monitor costs and clean up resources after practice. For beginners, step-by-step checklists would make independent learning and verification easier.

## Feedback questions and suggestions

### What satisfied me most

What satisfied me most was gradually seeing how services connect rather than treating them as isolated topics. Studying Cognito and Access Tokens helped me understand why APIs need to authenticate users; S3 and RDS gave me a better basis for considering how CloudDocs manages files and data. I was also pleased to complete my worklog and review my learning progress.

### What the program could improve

Add step-by-step integration labs, such as configuring IAM, connecting networks, signing in through Cognito and calling APIs, with sample errors for learners to diagnose.

Provide a checklist after each topic covering required settings, expected results and common errors.

Introduce evidence collection and worklog practices from the beginning so learners do not have to reconstruct everything at the end.

Allow time for learners to briefly present an architecture or completed lab and receive feedback from mentors and peers.

### Would I recommend the program to friends

Yes. I would recommend it to friends who want to learn AWS and are willing to study independently, read documentation and practice regularly. In my experience, the program helps students connect university knowledge with common components of cloud applications. Learners will make better progress if they actively complete labs and record what they have verified.

### Additional suggestions

Hold a session on cost estimation, configuring AWS Budgets and checking resources still running after practice.

Organize short peer sharing sessions to present architectures, encountered errors and solutions.

Add a checklist for backups, handover and AWS resource cleanup at the end of a lab or project.

### Would I like to stay involved in the program

Yes. I would like to continue learning through Cloud Journey, attend suitable meetups and apply my knowledge to CloudDocs. Going forward, I want to focus more on Backend and Cloud, particularly authorization, API authentication, service connectivity, integration testing and system monitoring.

### Acknowledgements

I thank the mentors and the First Cloud AI Journey community for sharing materials and experience and providing an environment for learning during my internship. The topics I studied gave me a clearer AWS foundation and helped me identify skills I need to keep developing. I will work to turn this knowledge into concrete practical exercises in the coming months.
